Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Greenfield

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
