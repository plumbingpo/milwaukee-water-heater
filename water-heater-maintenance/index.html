A water heater has two slow enemies: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they cut efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the damage is usually done. That is the reason maintenance matters for Milwaukee, WI homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Milwaukee Water Heater at (414) 209-3952 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest is harder than online videos make it seem. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

Our trucks are stocked for the usual surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring experience. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
